# Framing
The core analysis must treat the Iranian people and historical internal political forces as important drivers of long-term state transition, rather than treating them as passive spectators of external military action. A deterministic or linear "regime collapse" narratives driven solely by foreign intervention is likely but should not be all it is. The analysis must explicitly decouple a temporary lack of open street mobilization under heavy military repression from the actual, latent domestic support for broad transition coalitions.

# Research steering
Source De-escalation & Verification: Actively downplay single-source intelligence, emotional political rhetoric, or private sentiment leaks (such as claims of a leader's "frustration") from heavily invested external actors like Israel or the United States.

Diversification: Force the pipeline to search for, translate, and weigh indigenous Iranian voices, popular secular opposition statements, and independent tracking of labor strikes or civil disobedience inside Iran, their leader and what they want.

Recency & Rhetoric Filter: Filter political statements from the U.S. administration (specifically Trump) by cross-referencing past performance; separate transactional negotiating rhetoric from literal operational intent is spoken words.

# Chairman steering
The forum chairman must stress-test any assumption that external military actors (the US and Israel) and the domestic opposition have divergent long-term fates; they are currently operating under a structural alignment of necessity, fighting the same immediate adversary. The chairman must challenge external states trying to minimize the domestic popularity of broad transition coalitions (like the Pahlavi-aligned framework).
